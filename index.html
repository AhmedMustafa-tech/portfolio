Operations

The operation I run every day.

The automation only works because I know the day-to-day job. This is the operations side of my role.

Service desk, two regions

I work tickets across both regional queues every day: access requests, accounts, devices and mobile setup. It’s also where I find what to automate next.

Role · Own my queue

Onboarding waves & devices

I coordinate Egypt onboarding waves: collect the joiner list, get CRM, contact-centre and VPN accounts created by the owning teams, and allocate laptops. I flag upstream gaps, like late account requests, to HR.

Role · Coordinator for Egypt

Hardware procurement

Role · Initiator

Identity & access

Role · Operator

Incident coordination

Role · Incident owner / coordinator

Vendor escalations

I take problems to vendors with evidence: a device-MFA enforcement gap and a Windows biometric conflict with JumpCloud, and licensing paths through our Google reseller.

Role · Escalation owner
Troubleshooting

Find the real cause, and say so when it isn't proven.

DNS · mail routing · P0 incident

Customer replies bouncing after the domain migration

During a P0 blocklisting incident, support mail moved to a new sending subdomain. Outbound worked, but customer replies started bouncing with "domain not found". I traced it to the subdomain having SPF and DKIM for sending but no MX records for receiving. We verified the subdomain in Google Workspace, added MX and routing to forward replies into the existing inbox, and confirmed replies were landing again the next day.

Identity · MFA rollout

Locked out by their own shortcut

Midway through the company-wide device MFA rollout, two groups started failing to log in: people who had set up an authenticator alongside a security key and later deleted the app, and people who had only ever used Touch ID and no longer knew their password. I identified both patterns, built MFA-reset and credential-recovery procedures for them, and kept the rollout at zero permanent lockouts.

FAQ

Questions I get asked.

Why aren't there exact figures?

My employer's operational data isn't mine to publish, so I show scope and before → after ratios instead: how often, how many steps, how many regions. I'm happy to talk through results in an interview where that's appropriate.

Did you build these alone?

Yes. I designed, built and maintain Atlas, the dashboard, the offboarding automation and the smaller workflows myself. Colleagues gave feedback and approvals, and other teams owned the systems I integrated with.

How do you use AI?

In two ways. Atlas uses OpenAI GPT to answer queue questions and return approved procedures. And I use AI coding assistants to build faster. The design decisions, debugging and verification are mine.

What would you automate first on a new team?

The loop people repeat most by hand, usually SLA follow-up or offboarding. I'd map it step by step, automate the repeatable parts with read-only access first, and verify results at the destination before calling it done.
